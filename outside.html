Personal interests

Outside software engineering, my interests range from how markets work to how far I can get on two wheels.

The activity totals below are a snapshot from November 2026.

Computer Science

  • Every few years I revisit the classics. I keep a reading list of papers that helped define the field.
  • Lately I’ve been following data-efficient learning, long-context sequence modeling, and multi-agent systems. I’m fascinated by what models can learn from the distribution of their training data and what sampling from those models might help us discover.
  • I’m following work on teaching models to use tools, from software and operating systems to physical instruments, and how that might help automate scientific research.
  • I think demand for compute may follow Jevons’s paradox. The history of search and learning in AI is one reason I keep coming back to that idea.
  • On the systems side, I’m interested in orchestrating containers at scale, languages built around tensors with C++-class performance, and the relationship between data centers and power markets.

Finance & Capital Markets

  • I’m interested in quantitative trading and how markets have evolved alongside computing, especially exchange matching algorithms, price discovery, and auction design.
  • I enjoy reading about the history of commodity markets, from the Dojima rice exchange to the development of oil futures markets. I’m curious about how centralizing trade changed the way these markets developed.
  • I think prices and technological gains in productivity tell us a great deal about long-run economic growth and the stability of societies.
  • Beyond trading, I’m interested in deal-making, particularly private equity and venture investing.
